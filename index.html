Onuoha Obinna

Product engineer · Lagos, Nigeria

I build products end to end — the interface, the API, and the parts underneath that have to be right when money moves.

Most of what I make falls into two groups: marketplaces that connect people who need something done with people who can do it, and tools that take the tedium out of the unglamorous parts of software.

Right now I'm building Journey Studio, a canvas for API journeys. You chain HTTP calls together and the ids and tokens each step produces flow into the next. Import an OpenAPI document or a Postman collection, or just describe what you want in plain English and let it assemble the journey — then keep editing it by hand. Save one and it becomes a regression test that runs in CI and checks every response against the spec the API published.

And Crow, a local-services marketplace that lives inside Telegram. You describe what you need the way you'd say it out loud — English or Pidgin, typed or as a voice note — and Crow broadcasts the request to approved providers who match. The first one to accept gets the job, and both sides talk through the bot without ever trading phone numbers. A model reads the conversation; Postgres decides who gets the job and who gets paid.

Before those, CreatorMarkt: brands hire creators for video and photo work, the budget sits in escrow until the brand approves it, and the post is then tracked across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and X to see how it actually performed. And BetProphet, which prices football markets from its own models, finds the ones the bookmaker has wrong, and sizes the stake so a cold week doesn't end the experiment.

I've been writing code in public since 2018 — Flutter and Dart back then, mostly TypeScript and Python now. I'm drawn to problems where the hard part is correctness rather than scale: escrow, idempotency, who-accepted-first. And I'd rather build the whole thing than one slice of it.
